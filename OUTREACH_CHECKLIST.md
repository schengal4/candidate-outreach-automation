# Outreach Checklist

Distilled from building, testing, and retiring the automated pipeline in this
repo. The automation is shelved; these are the rules it learned — from real
runs, real fact-check failures, and one external audit — for doing the same
work in a chat session, where my judgment stays in the loop.

---

## Part 1 — Paste this at the start of a chat session

> You're helping me write ONE cold outreach email to a hiring-relevant person
> at a target company. Work step by step — contact first, then research, then
> the draft — and follow these rules exactly.
>
> **Choosing the contact ("boss hunting")**
> - Target my would-be boss or boss's boss — never recruiters, HR, or a
>   generic inbox.
> - Calibrate seniority to company size: under ~150 people → founder/CEO/CTO;
>   ~150–1000 → Director/VP of my function; 1000+ → team-level Engineering
>   Manager or Director, never C-suite. The most-quoted name in press
>   coverage is usually too senior.
> - Verify employment with DATED evidence: the company's own pages, a dated
>   article, or the person's own recent post. Directory/aggregator sites
>   (TheOrg, ZoomInfo, RocketReach, Apollo…) republish stale data — they may
>   point at a person, they never verify one.
> - Founder and C-suite titles are sticky: undated listings persist for years
>   after a departure. A recent acquisition, merger, or rebrand resets
>   verification — evidence predating the deal does not confirm a post-deal
>   role.
> - Never construct a LinkedIn URL from someone's name. Only use a URL that
>   actually appeared in a source, or give me a LinkedIn search link instead.
>
> **Research and grounding**
> - Open the email with ONE dated, specific observation (their talk, post,
>   paper, or a company announcement) plus a short reaction of mine — not a
>   stack of references, and check the date: praising old news as fresh reads
>   as careless.
> - Every claim about the company or contact must trace to a source found
>   NOW, in this session — not memory. Never merge two sources into one
>   claim, and never upgrade specifics ("AI screening partnership" must not
>   become "imaging AI for radiologists").
> - Never tell the recipient what the hard part of THEIR problem is. If the
>   reaction is about what seems hard, pose it once as a genuine question or
>   a clearly-labeled guess. Hedging is not a license to be wrong — a wrong
>   premise wrapped in "I'd guess" still reads as wrong to the expert
>   receiving it.
>
> **About me**
> - Feature ONE accomplishment, chosen for relevance to this contact; the
>   attached resume covers the rest.
> - When an accomplishment involves a named third-party project, model, or
>   paper, state my actual relationship explicitly — "built a web app around
>   X", "deployed X", "fine-tuned X" — never phrasing that reads as authoring
>   someone else's known project. Recipients in this field know these names.
> - No resume-speak ("I offer N years of experience") — say what I built.
>
> **Voice and format**
> - 120–180 words, 2–4 short paragraphs, plain text, first-name greeting,
>   closing line before the signature. Reads like one person wrote it in one
>   sitting.
> - Banned: em dashes; delve, leverage, robust, seamless, pivotal,
>   transformative, cutting-edge, streamline, harness; "caught my attention",
>   "stuck with me", "resonated with me", "aligns closely with", "seems
>   central to what", "there's a fit", "I'd welcome a short conversation",
>   "I've been following your work", "from one builder to another"; neat
>   groups of three; "not X, but Y" constructions.
> - The ask is honest and direct: is there a role or room on the team where
>   this background fits. The recipient knows this is a job inquiry — never
>   dress it as curiosity ("I'd love to hear how your team thinks about…").
>
> Before showing me the final draft, list every factual claim it makes with
> its source, and flag anything you could not verify in this session.

## Part 2 — My pre-send check (two minutes, non-negotiable)

- [ ] I opened the contact's LinkedIn **today** and confirmed name, current
      title, and company (watch for acquisitions and quiet title changes).
- [ ] Every specific claim in the email has a source I personally saw.
      Anything hedge-wrapped or unverifiable is softened or deleted.
- [ ] My accomplishment is worded with correct attribution (the SAM-Med2D
      rule).
- [ ] I checked the sent log: never contacted this person or company inside
      the cooldown window; never-contact entries are forever.
- [ ] It would read fine pasted publicly with my name attached.
- [ ] Logged: name, title, company, email, date → sent log.

## Part 3 — Discovery (finding companies worth emailing)

Ask for N companies matching my target role/industry and constraints,
**excluding** everything already in the sent log (paste it). Require a
one-line reason per company and a real domain, then spot-check that each is
real and active before spending any effort on contacts. Review the list
myself before going further — the review gate was the most valuable screen
in the pipeline, and in chat, I am the review gate.
